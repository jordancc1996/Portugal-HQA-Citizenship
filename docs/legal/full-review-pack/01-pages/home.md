## What the HQA visa actually is

**P9.** "HQA" stands for highly qualified activity. It is not the name of a law or a government program. It is a label for two legal tools:

**P10.** The D3 residence visa (article 61), which lets you enter Portugal to carry out highly qualified work.

**P11.** The residence permit for highly qualified activity (article 90), which lets you stay and renew.

**P12.** These tools were built for skilled workers, researchers and founders. An investment package is one way to qualify, not the only one. A job contract with a Portuguese employer that meets the salary and qualification rules can lead to the same permit with no investment at all.

## Frequently asked questions

**P61.** Is the HQA visa a real visa?

**P62.** The underlying visa and permit are real and are set out in articles 61 and 90 of Lei 23/2007. "HQA visa" is an informal label, not an official program name.

**P63.** Is €175,000 the official minimum?

**P64.** No. Article 90 does not set a minimum investment payment. Some private providers sell packages that include company and research-project services; their price and scope are provider-specific. €175,000 is reported by providers, October 2026. No government body sets or checks it.

**P65.** How long until I can get Portuguese citizenship?

**P66.** Under Lei Orgânica 1/2026, in force since 19 May 2026, most applicants need 10 years of legal residence. EU nationals and nationals of countries where Portuguese is an official language need 7.

**P67.** Can I bring my family?

**P68.** Yes. Family members of article 90 permit holders are exempt from the 2-year waiting period that applies to most other permits since October 2025.

**P69.** Do I need to live in Portugal?

**P70.** The law does not set a number of days you must be in Portugal. A temporary permit may be cancelled after more than 6 consecutive months, or 8 non-consecutive months, outside Portugal during the permit's total validity, unless the absence is justified.
